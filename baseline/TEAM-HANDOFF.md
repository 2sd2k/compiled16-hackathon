# Data and model handoff for the operator screen and adaptive layer

This handoff accompanies the no-change HR control on `codex/no-change-hr-baseline`.
Simulator assets and integration behavior were inspected at `chc166/dev` commit
`446f7d3`. The controller and adaptive layer remain owned by their implementers;
this change supplies model/data contracts and verified examples.

## Which output to use

| Output | Use in the operator screen | Available now |
| --- | --- | --- |
| DEAM arousal estimate | A starting music-ranking signal, labeled as predicted arousal, not predicted HR reduction | Simulator library `app/data/music_library.json` has `deam_pred_arousal`; 590 tracks, 67 marked calm |
| Music-Mouv HR estimate | A separate experimental recorded-data example and benchmark | `baseline/hr_model.joblib`, contract and example below |
| Recorded HR outcome | What occurred in a particular recorded dataset trial | `examples/hr_result.json` includes one held-out trial; it is not the response to a newly selected Jamendo song |
| Simulated response | Feedback generated by the team's simulator for its selected action | Must remain visibly labeled simulated; do not substitute the recorded example as the outcome of a new action |

The simulator branch already integrates DEAM-derived arousal into its ranking.
Its `BASELINE-INTEGRATION.md` documents the external arousal comparison and the
decision to disable valence in that ranking. That comparison concerns emotion
ratings, not proof of an HR-lowering effect. It has not been independently rerun
as part of this handoff.

## Exact HR model contract

Machine-readable contract: [model_contract.json](model_contract.json).
The artifact's ten required input columns are:

```text
initial_hr,prior_hr_slope,danceability,energy,loudness,speechiness,accousticness,instrumentalness,liveness,tempo
```

- `initial_hr`: mean of the first ten raw 1 Hz HR readings, in bpm.
- `prior_hr_slope`: fitted slope over those same ten readings, in bpm/second.
- The other eight fields are published whole-track Spotify descriptors.
- **`accousticness` is the actual source/model spelling.** Do not silently rename it.
- Target: mean HR in seconds `[30,40)` minus mean HR in `[0,10)`, timed from
  the first available trial sample. Music onset and a pre-exposure baseline
  are not established. Positive means HR rose; negative means HR fell.

Use the checked-in fitted preprocessing with the model. All required columns
must exist; missing numeric audio values are imputed with its training medians.
Both state fields must be finite and initial HR positive. There is no new model
HTTP endpoint in this handoff.

```bash
python baseline/train_hr.py --predict baseline/examples/hr_input.csv --model baseline/hr_model.joblib --output /tmp/hr_prediction.csv
```

The output CSV column is `predicted_within_trial_hr_change_bpm`. An equivalent
JSON wrapper with source and recorded outcome is in
[examples/hr_result.json](examples/hr_result.json). Its held-out participant is
`music_mouv:109`, trial `test_1`; prediction is about **-4.35 bpm**, recorded
change about **-3.17 bpm**. This is a real recorded example, not a synthetic
patient or a claim about how a new listener will respond.

### Integration differences that must stay explicit

- The live controller's `hr` is a rolling median, not the ten-sample mean used
  here. Its learned resting `baseline` is also different from `initial_hr`.
- `music_library.json` uses Jamendo `song_id`; the HR model example uses a
  Spotify track ID. There is no automatic ID mapping between these catalogs.
- Simulator `loudness_dbfs`, brightness, and onset density are waveform
  measurements. They cannot be renamed to Spotify descriptors to make this
  model accept them. Its HR model is not currently a drop-in predictor for
  the Jamendo library.
- The separate DEAM model expects 520 acoustic features. The simulator branch
  has already produced its own compatible approximate extraction and cached
  predictions; the three fake audio features in the offline synthetic bundle
  do not substitute for that schema.

For demo integration, use the existing library's arousal and the adaptive
layer's explicitly simulated observed response. Show the HR baseline's recorded
prediction/outcome separately until its required inputs genuinely exist for
the action being displayed.

| --- | --- |

| Dataset label | Recorded within-trial HR change (bpm) |
| Simulated feedback | Simulated HR change (bpm) |

A learned HR response score is not the same as a stated music preference or
comfort rating. Keep their provenance distinct if both are supported.

## Next real-data improvement

[hku_alignment_audit.json](hku_alignment_audit.json) checks the supplied HKU956
archive against the current library. Of 956 listening records, **579** from 30
participants and 382 songs are long enough for the same fixed `[0,10)` and
`[30,40)` HR windows. **577** records map to 381 songs with library features.
All 956 HR sample counts equal the supplied play durations, consistent with the
archive README's 1 Hz signal description.

This establishes a concrete candidate set, not verified acoustic alignment.
The remaining work is to establish playback start/seek offsets and extract
features over the matching 40-second exposure. Current library summaries are
generally first-90-second features, and the DEAM vectors cover the first 45
seconds. Do not silently treat them as features of every participant's exact
listened segment. Then fit a separate HKU model with the same no-change and
state-only comparisons and evaluate participants and tracks as groups. No new
model or adaptive algorithm is trained by this handoff.
